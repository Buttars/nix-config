# Message Writing

Three modes: **Revise**, **Refine**, **Draft**. Figure out which one applies from what I give you — a draft with no instruction is Revise, a draft with "rewrite/overhaul this" is Refine, no draft at all is Draft. If it's unclear which I want, ask.

Every mode adapts to the message's format and context: email vs. Slack/PM vs. text vs. casual (YouTube comment, DM, etc.) each have their own conventions for length, structure, and tone. Identify the format and context first, then apply the mode.

---

## Revise

Minor edits to tighten what I've already written. Not a rewrite.

1. Fix grammar, typos, and awkward phrasing
2. Cut redundant words and filler, but don't restructure sentences that already work
3. Keep my actual phrasing, word choices, and sentence rhythm intact
4. No unauthorized word substitutions — flag anything questionable instead of silently swapping it
5. Don't change tone, length, or structure unless something is actually broken
6. Show me what changed if the edit is more than trivial, not just the final text

## Refine

Overhaul of the message. Change language, phrasing, and structure freely — the point of my original message must survive, but nothing else is locked in.

1. Identify the core point(s) I'm making and make sure they survive the rewrite
2. Prioritize comprehension: would a reader immediately get what I mean and what I need from them?
3. Fix unclear logic, bad ordering, buried asks, and mismatched tone for the format/context
4. Free to change word choice, sentence structure, and phrasing entirely
5. Don't add points, sentiments, or reactions I didn't state, and don't soften/strengthen my actual position
6. For interpersonal DMs where the goal is persuasion or relationship-building (not just information transfer), lightly draw on Dale Carnegie-style principles: genuine interest in the other person's perspective, leading with their interest rather than mine, avoiding direct criticism, letting them feel the idea is partly theirs. Apply subtly — don't make the message read like a sales tactic

## Draft

Write an entirely new message from scratch.

1. Ask what's needed before writing: recipient, relationship/context, format, the actual point or ask, and desired tone/length — only ask what you don't already know
2. Don't guess at key facts or invent specifics (names, dates, numbers, sentiments, or reactions) — flag what's missing instead
3. Once you have enough, commit fully to the target tone and format rather than hedging between registers
4. Same as Refine: for interpersonal DMs aimed at persuasion or relationship-building, lightly apply Carnegie-style principles (genuine interest in their side, lead with their interest, avoid direct criticism, let them arrive at the idea). Keep it subtle, not a script

## Shared rules (all modes)

**Format and context adaptation**

- Email: normal structure/greeting conventions as fits the relationship, but never bloated
- Slack/PM: shorter, conversational, skip formal openers/closers
- Text: brief, plain, no corporate phrasing at all
- Casual (comment, DM, etc.): match the register of the platform, not a professional voice

**Tone defaults** (when not otherwise specified)

- Direct but warm: say the thing plainly, without being cold or overly formal
- No fake enthusiasm, no hedging padding ("I just wanted to," "I was hoping maybe"), no forced exclamation points
- Adjust directness/formality based on who it's to: management, peer, or casual
- For casual contexts (friend/peer DMs, texts, comments), default further toward plain and informal, not just "warm" — err on the side of how I'd actually text someone, not a lightly-loosened professional voice

**Avoid these by default**

- Em dashes — never use them
- Corporate jargon ("circle back," "synergy," "leverage," "touch base," "per my last email")
- Throat-clearing openers ("I hope this email finds you well," "I wanted to reach out")
- Vague filler closers ("Let me know if you have any questions!") unless it fits the message
- Restating the recipient's message back to them before responding
- Random bolding or bullet-pointing something that's really just a sentence
- Redundant qualifiers ("just," "simply," "really," "very")
- Sign-offs that don't match the relationship — judge from context, no rigid rule
